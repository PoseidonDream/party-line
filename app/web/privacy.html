Privacy policy

Last updated 29 October 2026

Party Line is a private chat and voice app for a group of friends. This copy of Party Line runs on its own server, set up by the person who runs it for the group (“the server owner”). Nobody else, including whoever wrote the software, can see what’s on it. This page explains what the server keeps about you, why, and how to get it deleted.

Questions about your data: ask an admin of this Party Line in the app.

What the server keeps

  • Your account: your name and your password. The password is stored only as a scrambled fingerprint (scrypt), so nobody can read it.
  • Your email, if you add one: only to send you codes (to confirm it, and to reset a forgotten password). Nobody else sees it, and it’s never used for anything else.
  • Your birth date, if you confirm your age: only to open channels marked 18+. Nobody sees it; the app only uses whether you’re 18 or older.
  • Your profile, if you fill it in: picture, “About me”, banner, status, favorite games and birthday (day and month only).
  • What you send: messages, photos, videos, files, voice messages, reactions, polls, events and soundboard sounds, so your friends can see them later. Disappearing messages are deleted when their time is up.
  • Who you know: your friends, the servers and group chats you’re in, and who you’ve blocked.
  • Devices you sign in on: a sign-in key for each device, the kind of device (like “Android app”) and when it was last used. If you turn on notifications while Party Line is closed, the address your browser or phone gives for them.
  • How the app is used: for each feature, how many times it was used per person per day (for example “3 polls”), so the server owner sees what’s worth keeping. Never what anyone said.
  • Reports: if you report a message or a person, the report, with your name and a copy of the message.

Voice, camera and screen sharing

Party Line uses your microphone only while you’re in a voice channel or recording a voice message, and your camera or screen only when you turn them on. Voice, camera and screen sharing go straight between you and your friends, or through this server’s own media server for big channels. They are not recorded. When someone uses “Clip it” on a screen share, the last 30 seconds of it are saved as a video in the chat, like any other video.

Why

Only to run Party Line for your group: showing your messages to the people you sent them to, connecting voice, notifying you, keeping your account safe, and making nightly backups so nothing is lost if the server breaks. Nothing is sold, used for ads, or shared with anyone outside the group.

Other services involved

  • Email codes (if the server owner set up email) go out through the email service they chose, for example Gmail.
  • Notifications while Party Line is closed travel through your browser’s or phone’s push service (Google, Apple, Mozilla or Microsoft). They can’t read what’s inside.
  • Link previews: when you send a link, the server fetches that page to show a preview, so the site sees the server, not you.
  • GIF search (if the server owner turned it on) sends your search words to Giphy.
  • /ask (if the server owner turned it on) sends your question to Anthropic’s Claude AI to get the answer.
  • Backups may be copied to a storage service the server owner chose, so they survive if the server is lost. They’re kept for up to 30 days.

How long it’s kept, and deleting it

Things stay until you delete them, or until you delete your account. You can delete any message you sent. To delete your account and everything that’s yours (messages, private chats, photos, files, sounds): in the app, Settings → Account → Delete my account, or on the web at /delete-account. It happens straight away. Copies in backups disappear as old backups are removed, within 30 days.

Keeping it safe

The connection to the server is encrypted (HTTPS). Passwords are stored scrambled. You can turn on 2-step sign-in and see and sign out the devices you’re signed in on in Settings → Account.

Children

Party Line is not meant for children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version appears on this page with a new date.
