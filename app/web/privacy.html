Privacy policy

Last updated 1 November 2026

Party Line is a private chat and voice app for a group of friends. This copy of Party Line runs on its own server, set up by the person who runs it for the group (“the server owner”). Nobody else, including whoever wrote the software, can see what’s on it. This page explains what the server keeps about you, why, for how long, and what you can do about it. See also the Terms of service, the Community rules and the Cookie policy.

Who is responsible

The server owner decides what this Party Line is used for and is responsible for your data here (the “data controller”).

Questions about your data: ask an admin of this Party Line in the app.

What the server keeps

  • Your account: your name and your password. The password is stored only as a scrambled fingerprint (scrypt), so nobody can read it. Also when you agreed to these terms, and which version.
  • Your email, if you add one: only to send you codes (to confirm it, and to reset a forgotten password). Nobody else sees it, and it’s never used for anything else, like newsletters or ads.
  • Your birth date, if you confirm your age: to open channels marked 18+, and for your birthday: on the day, friends see 🎂 next to your name and Party Bot says happy birthday. Nobody sees the year or your age.
  • Your profile, if you fill it in: picture, background picture, “About me”, banner, status and favorite games.
  • What you send: messages, photos, videos, files, voice messages, reactions, polls, events and soundboard sounds, so your friends can see them later. Disappearing messages are deleted when their time is up.
  • Who you know: your friends, the servers and group chats you’re in, and who you’ve blocked.
  • Devices you sign in on: a sign-in key for each device, the kind of device (like “Android app”) and when it was last used. If you turn on notifications while Party Line is closed, the address your browser or phone gives for them.
  • How the app is used: for each feature, how many times it was used per person per day (for example “3 polls”), so the server owner sees what’s worth keeping. Never what anyone said.
  • Reports: if you report a message or a person, the report, with your name and a copy of the message.

Not kept: your IP address. The server only holds it for a moment, in memory, to stop people guessing passwords. Party Line has no ads, no tracking and no analytics services, and doesn’t ask for your location, contacts or phone number. The server’s technical logs (for example the voice relay’s, which can include IP addresses) are small and roll off automatically within days.

Voice, camera and screen sharing

Party Line uses your microphone only while you’re in a voice channel or recording a voice message, and your camera or screen only when you turn them on. Voice, camera and screen sharing go straight between you and your friends, or through this server’s own media server or relay. They are not recorded. When someone uses “Clip it” on a screen share, the last 30 seconds of it are saved as a video in the chat, like any other video.

Why, and on what basis

  • To run Party Line for you (what you signed up for): your account, showing your messages to the people you sent them to, connecting voice, notifications.
  • To keep it safe and working (the group’s legitimate interest): limits against password guessing and floods, reports, nightly backups, and the feature counts.
  • Because you chose to (your consent, which you can take back any time): your email, your birth date, notifications while Party Line is closed, and videos from YouTube.

Nothing is sold, used for ads, or shared with anyone outside the group.

Other services involved

Party Line itself has no third-party code that tracks you. These outside services are only involved when you use the feature:

  • YouTube (Google), only if you allow it: “Listen together” and video previews play through YouTube’s player, which can set YouTube’s own cookies. Until you allow it, nothing loads from YouTube. See the Cookie policy.
  • Notifications while Party Line is closed travel through your browser’s or phone’s push service (Google, Apple, Mozilla or Microsoft). They can’t read what’s inside.
  • Email codes (if the server owner set up email) go out through the email service they chose, for example Gmail.
  • Link previews: when you send a link, the server fetches that page to show a preview, so the site sees the server, not you.
  • GIF search (if the server owner turned it on) sends your search words to Giphy, through the server.
  • /ask (if the server owner turned it on) sends your question to Anthropic’s Claude AI, through the server.
  • App updates: the desktop app, and the Android app when it wasn’t installed from Google Play, check GitHub for a newer version. If you installed Party Line from Google Play, Google’s own terms cover the Play Store.
  • Backups may be copied to a storage service the server owner chose, so they survive if the server is lost.

Some of these companies may handle data in other countries.

How long it’s kept

  • Your account, profile and what you send: until you delete them, or delete your account.
  • Sign-ins: a device that hasn’t opened Party Line for 4 months is signed out and forgotten.
  • Feature counts: 30 days. Handled reports: 90 days. Password-reset requests: 7 days. The admins’ log: 6 months.
  • Backups: the newest 7 nightly backups on the server, and copies elsewhere for up to 30 days.

Your rights

  • See and take your data: Settings → Account → Download my data gives you a file with your account, profile and everything you sent.
  • Correct it: change your name, profile, email and password in Settings. An admin can fix a wrong birth date.
  • Delete it: delete any message you sent, or your whole account and everything that’s yours: in the app, Settings → Account → Delete my account, or on the web at /delete-account. It happens straight away; copies in backups disappear as old backups are removed, within 30 days.
  • Take back a choice: remove your email, turn off notifications, or stop YouTube in Settings → Account → Privacy.
  • Object or complain: ask an admin. You can also complain to your data protection authority (in Turkey: Kişisel Verileri Koruma Kurumu; in the EU: your country’s authority).

Keeping it safe

The connection to the server is encrypted (HTTPS). Passwords are stored scrambled. You can turn on 2-step sign-in and see and sign out the devices you’re signed in on in Settings → Account.

Children

Party Line is not for children under 13. If you’re under the age your country sets for agreeing to this yourself, ask a parent first. Channels marked 18+ open only for people who confirmed they’re 18 or older.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version appears on this page with a new date, and the app asks you to look at it.
